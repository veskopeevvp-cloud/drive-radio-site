Internet radio in the car with Android Auto

Vesko from Drive Radio

If your car has Android Auto, you already know how handy it is: navigation and music on the big screen, the phone somewhere out of the way. But the car's own radio only picks up local FM stations, and once you leave town they start crackling.

Internet radio fixes that. A station sounds just as clean in the city, on the motorway or across the border, as long as you have mobile data.

What it looks like in Android Auto

Drive Radio shows up among the media apps on the car's screen. You see the song title, the artist and the album art. You change stations with the steering wheel buttons or on the screen, without touching the phone.

I've tried it in several cars. What I like most is the split screen: Waze on the left, and on the right a small card with the song and the buttons.

And if the car has no Android Auto?

No problem. Drive Radio also works with a regular Bluetooth head unit: the sound comes through the car's speakers, and the song shows on the display if the car supports it. The radio still starts on its own as soon as you connect.

Tunnel, mountains, weak signal?

It happens: you drive into a tunnel or through a spot with no coverage and the internet is gone for a moment. Before, you had to pick up the phone afterwards and press "Play". Not anymore. As soon as the signal is back, Drive Radio reconnects by itself and the radio carries on. You just drive.

How much data does it use?

Less than you might think. Most stations stream at 128 kbps, which comes to about 55–60 MB per hour of listening. With a monthly plan of a few GB, that's perfectly fine for a daily commute.

Try it in your car. Listening is free.
